*** Can you explain the Damkoehler Criterion?



**Damköhler Criterion – quick overview*

| Aspect | What it is | Typical formula | Meaning |
|--------|------------|------------------|---------|
| *Damköhler number (Da)* | A dimensionless group that compares the rate of chemical reaction to the rate of transport (mixing, diffusion, convection). | \(\displaystyle Da = \frac{\text{characteristic reaction time}^{-1}}{\text{characteristic transport time}^{-1}} = \frac{k\,L^n}{U}\) (for a first‑order reaction in a flow of length \(L\), velocity \(U\), and rate constant \(k\); \(n\) depends on reaction order) | *Da ≫ 1* → reaction is fast relative to transport (reaction‑limited regime). <br>*Da ≪ 1* → transport is fast relative to reaction (mixing‑limited regime). |
| *Damköhler criterion* | The rule‑of‑thumb that a reacting flow will sustain a flame or undergo ignition only when the Damköhler number exceeds a critical value (often ≈ 1). | \(Da > Da_{\text{crit}}\) (≈ 1 for many simple systems) | If the reaction can proceed faster than the fluid can carry reactants away, the system can “catch fire” or maintain a reacting zone. |

*** Why it matters
- *Combustion / flame stability* – A flame will extinguish if turbulence or flow removes reactants faster than they can react (low Da). Conversely, a high Da means the mixture can burn locally, sustaining a flame.
- *Chemical reactors* – Determines whether a reactor is *mixing‑controlled* (low Da, need better stirring) or *kinetically‑controlled* (high Da, focus on catalyst/temperature).
- *Environmental & atmospheric chemistry* – Helps assess whether pollutant formation is limited by reaction chemistry or by transport (e.g., plume dispersion).

*** Typical regimes
| Regime | Da range | Dominant physics |
|--------|----------|------------------|
| *Transport‑limited* | \(Da \ll 1\) | Species are mixed/diffused faster than they react. Reaction appears “slow”. |
| *Intermediate* | \(Da \approx 1\) | Reaction and transport occur on comparable time scales; small changes tip the system toward ignition or extinction. |
| *Reaction‑limited* | \(Da \gg 1\) | Reactants are consumed as soon as they meet; overall rate set by how fast they can be brought together (e.g., by diffusion through a thin flame sheet). |

*** Simple example (plug‑flow reactor)

For a first‑order reaction \(A \rightarrow B\) with rate constant \(k\) in a tubular reactor of length \(L\) and average velocity \(U\):

\[
Da = \frac{kL}{U}
\]

- If \(Da = 0.1\) → only ~10 % conversion (transport dominates).  
- If \(Da = 10\) → > 99 % conversion (reaction dominates).  

The *Damköhler criterion* would state that to achieve a target conversion (e.g., > 90 %), you need \(Da\) above the critical value that yields that conversion.

*** Bottom line
- *Damköhler number* quantifies the competition between chemistry and flow.  
- *Criterion*: sustaining a reacting zone (flame, ignition, high conversion) generally requires \(Da\) above a critical threshold—often close to unity.  
- Adjusting *reaction rates* (temperature, catalyst) or *transport rates* (mixing, velocity, diffusion length) moves the system across the criterion.

def test_reaction_stability(D, dx, dC_dx, w, f, dt_current, C_local):
    """
    D: Diffusion coefficient [m^2/s]
    dx: Grid cell size [m]
    dC_dx: Concentration gradient [mol/m^4]
    w: Advection velocity [m/s]
    f: Reaction rate [mol/m^3/s]
    dt_current: Your intended solver time step [s]
    C_local: Typical concentration in the reaction zone [mol/m^3]
    """
    
    # 1. Characteristic Time Scales
    # t_reaction (tau): Time to consume the local concentration pool
    t_reaction = C_local / f if f > 0 else np.inf
    
    # t_transport: Combined time for diffusion and advection to clear a cell
    t_diffusion = (dx**2) / (2 * D)
    t_advection = dx / w if w > 0 else np.inf
    t_transport = 1.0 / (1.0/t_diffusion + 1.0/t_advection)
    
    # 2. Damköhler Number (II) - Reaction vs Diffusion
    da_ii = f / (D * dC_dx / dx + 1e-20)
